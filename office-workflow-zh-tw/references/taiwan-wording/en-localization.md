# zh-TW ⇄ English localization notes (original, v0.3.0)

Compiled by this project from general localization practice and public style guidance. All examples are our own.

## 1. zh-TW → English

**Politeness formulas: translate the function, not the words**

| zh-TW | Avoid | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 麻煩您… | "Trouble you…" | "Could you please…", "When you get a chance, could you…" |
| 敬請見諒 | "Kindly be forgiven" | "Thanks for your patience", "Sorry for the inconvenience" |
| 感謝您撥冗 | "Thank you for squeezing time" | "Thanks for taking the time to reply" |
| 再請不吝告知 | "Please do not be stingy to tell" | "Let me know if you have any questions" |
| 辛苦了 | "You worked hard" | "Thanks for all your work on this", "Appreciate it!" |
| 不好意思 | always "I'm embarrassed" | "Excuse me" / "Sorry about that" / "Sorry to bother you" (by context) |
| 請多指教 | "Please give me more guidance" | "Looking forward to working with you" |
| 王先生／王經理 | "Mr. Wang Manager" | "Mr. Wang" (or first name if the relationship is casual) |

**Chinglish to avoid:** "add oil" (加油 → "You've got this"), "people mountain people sea" (人山人海 → "packed", "super crowded"), "open the light" (開燈 → "turn on the light"), "discuss about", "very like", "kindly" repeated, "cost-effective" for casual 划算 (→ "great value").

**Casual zh-TW → casual English**
- Use contractions; short, uneven sentences; one detail and one reaction.
- Particles become tone, not words: 啦 → "honestly", "just"; 喔 → "btw", a friendly "oh"; 欸 → "hey"; 吧 → "right?", "I guess".
- Emoji in the source stay; do not add more.
- Map slang by meaning: 踩雷 → "total letdown", "a miss"; 普普 → "meh", "just okay"; CP值超低 → "terrible value for money"; 不會回購 → "won't be back"; 好吃到哭 → "so good I could cry".
- Only use English slang (ngl, lowkey, slaps) if the source is that casual and the audience is young; at most one term.

**Names, numbers, units**
- Dishes: romanized name + short gloss on first mention: lu rou fan (braised pork rice), taro balls, beef noodle soup. Keep well-known English names (bubble tea, xiaolongbao).
- Places: use established spellings (Taipei, Tainan, Kaohsiung, Jiufen, Hsinchu). Districts: Tainan's West Central District.
- Money: NT$1,490 (spell out "New Taiwan dollars" once only if readers may not know). 3萬 = 30,000; 1億 = 100 million.
- Dates: 11/15 → "November 15". 民國 year + 1911 = Western year (民國115年 = 2026).
- Units: 坪 ≈ 3.3 m²; 台斤 = 600 g. Convert only if the reader needs it; keep the original number in parentheses.

## 2. English → zh-TW

- Remove English marketing scaffolding instead of translating it. "We're thrilled/excited/proud to announce" is never translated as 我們很高興宣布／很高興地推出; use 全新推出／正式開賣 or start with the product; "seamless", "elevate", "game-changer" → say the concrete benefit or cut.
- Keep numbers, prices, dates, limits, and platform names exactly. 600 ml → 600 ml or 600 毫升 (keep the source style); "up to 30 days" → 最長 30 天 (not 30 天).
- Casual English → casual zh-TW: "lol" → 哈哈／XD, "tmr" → 明天, "ngl" → 老實說, "slaps" → 超好喝／超讚, "lowkey" → 默默覺得／其實. Do not translate slang word for word.
- "Free shipping in Taiwan" → 台灣本島免運 only if the source says 本島; otherwise 台灣免運.

## 3. AI-sounding English to remove (zh-TW → English output)

- Words: delve, tapestry, elevate, seamless, leverage, robust, unlock, landscape, realm, vibrant, bustling, embark, pivotal, game-changer, journey (figurative).
- Openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Whether you're X or Y", "Let's dive in", "In conclusion".
- Patterns: "not only… but also", forced lists of three, em dashes everywhere, a moral at the end of every paragraph.
